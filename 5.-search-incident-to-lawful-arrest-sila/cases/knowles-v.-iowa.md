# Knowles v. Iowa

## Case Summary: Knowles v. Iowa (1998)

### Key Facts

* Patrick Knowles was stopped for speeding (43 mph in a 25 mph zone) in Newton, Iowa
* Officer issued a citation instead of arresting him
* Officer then conducted a full search of the vehicle and found marijuana and drug paraphernalia under the driver's seat
* Knowles was arrested and charged with controlled substance violations

### Legal Issue

Does Iowa law authorizing a full vehicle search incident to issuing a traffic citation violate the Fourth Amendment's protection against unreasonable searches?

### Iowa's Position

* Iowa Code § 805.1(4) authorized officers to conduct searches even when issuing citations instead of making arrests
* Iowa Supreme Court had created a "search incident to citation" exception, arguing that probable cause to arrest was sufficient—actual arrest unnecessary

### Supreme Court's Ruling

**Unanimous decision: The search was unconstitutional**

The Court reversed the Iowa Supreme Court, holding that the "search incident to arrest" exception does not extend to situations where only a citation is issued.

### Reasoning

The Court examined the two traditional justifications for search incident to arrest:

1. **Officer Safety**: While legitimate, the threat is significantly less during a routine traffic stop than a custodial arrest. Officers already have adequate protections (ordering occupants out, Terry frisks, etc.) without full search authority.
2. **Preserving Evidence**: Once the speeding citation was issued, all necessary evidence for that offense had been obtained. No additional evidence would be found in the vehicle.

The Court declined to extend the "bright-line rule" from _Robinson_ to citations, where the safety and evidence concerns are minimal or absent.
